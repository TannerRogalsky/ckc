#### The pyramid and the souls in the tome

[[The Opal]] continued east toward [[Broy]], carrying [[Theo Harvey]] as a prisoner. The waters approached familiar old-world landscapes: ancient trees still stood where rising seas had submerged their roots, and the surviving heights looked less transformed than some of the regions the party had crossed. [[Kerben]] struggled with the demands of steering, occasionally relieved by [[Red Caesar]]. His acting command remained tied to the hope of recovering [[Obould]], whose place aboard the ship the crew still respected.

[[Ceril]] used Commune with Nature to search for the fiend holding the [[Tome of Satariel]]. Dragonflies appeared as signs of a nearby fiendish presence, while a vision of the surrounding terrain revealed an unnatural pyramid in the eastern waters. It had been constructed through magic by a single being within the preceding few decades. Ceril marked the position of [[Tome Keeper's Pyramid]] on the map, giving the party a practical lead on the unnamed [[Arcanoloth]] they had discussed finding.

The discovery revived Ceril's proposed leverage over [[Keys Caeradel]]. The [[Rakshasa]] had said that the tome's keeper hunted him; Ceril had considered compelling that enemy into service and bringing the threat to Keys Caeradel's father, hoping to influence the family's plans for the [[Demi-Spell]] and its use of [[Penumbra]]. That remained a proposal. The party had neither captured the fiend nor settled how to make such a bargain work.

[[Vokenar]] invoked divine intervention to learn the tome's history through Legend Lore. His eyes rolled back, and he spoke as a conduit for knowledge that he could hear himself delivering. The revelation described a temple built specifically to contain the book, an infernal tabernacle whose interior retained its arrangement from hell.

The tome had become a permanent artifact after its keeper murdered 666 wizards and extracted their souls to create its 666 original pages. It might contain more souls by now. Destroying it in the material world would return it to hell, where it would be less dangerous to [[Stark]]. While it remained above, its keeper could use it to capture more souls, especially those of magical beings. Demonic souls held greater value in hell, helping explain why the Rakshasa was a desirable target. The keeper could not acquire arcane knowledge by learning: it gained knowledge through death. The temple would also likely contain traps and other dangers placed by its possessor.

That account unsettled Ceril's plan to work with the fiend. The party now had good reason to regard it as an unequivocally dangerous partner, and Ceril asked how they could protect their own souls. Red Caesar's abjurations could offer some protection, but there was no established way to make the party completely safe from the book. They considered Magic Jar as a means of separating a soul from its body, then recognized that it was outside their present capabilities and would not let an emptied body simply continue fighting.

Planar Binding offered a more practical possibility: they might compel the fiend into service long enough to speak with it and explain a bargain. They also discussed banishment and the reliability of the Rakshasa's warning that the keeper could not be reasoned with. Red Caesar allowed that the warning might reflect the Rakshasa's own interests, while reminding Ceril that the tome's history supplied independent reasons for caution. None of these spells was used against the keeper.

The party still believed it had only about sixteen days before Obould's threatened execution. Entering the pyramid without adequate preparation could cost them their souls and the chance to rescue him. They chose to continue toward Broy, leaving the pyramid for later and considering whether they could obtain useful spells before returning. Kerben drove his point home by stabbing the map and insisting that they proceed. Ceril's suggestion of leaving a familiar to watch the site remained uncarried out.

#### Theo Harvey's account of The Karat Cake

During Domyx's night watch, Theo Harvey tried to obtain better treatment, asking about more comfortable accommodation and rum. [[Domyx]] kept the party's business guarded and declined to supply alcohol that might become a weapon. He did not accept Theo Harvey's attempt to describe them as friends; friendship, to him, required loyalty.

Theo Harvey claimed that the [[Broyish Empire]] would execute him for failing his assignment, possibly branding him a traitor as a public display of control regardless of how much useful information he possessed. Domyx answered with confidence that the party could protect him. Their recent defeat of Theo Harvey and his imperial escort gave that assurance substance, and the prisoner became more willing to offer information. They did not agree to release him.

Theo Harvey warned that another member of [[Clan Akathia]] was in Broy. He then described [[The Karat Cake]] as far more dangerous and valuable than an ordinary abandoned amusement park. [[Jack Harvey]] had powered it with Penumbra before the sky broke and had understood the material's connection to the heavens. The park contained a maze of illusions, arcane traps, and magic capable of transporting people across the world.

According to Theo Harvey, [[Vizier Jade]] had sent [[The Tyrant]], a huge, bulky creature with a single central eye, to find the park. It had never returned. He presented the maze as a place that could trap anyone entering it, while also imagining its surviving stores and defenses as the basis for a refuge or a rich discovery. He knew something of its whereabouts, but did not provide a precise route during this conversation. His references to the Empire's plans for falling waters were less clear, and did not establish how those plans worked.

Domyx remained cautious rather than accepting the prisoner's account as an alliance. Theo Harvey eventually returned to bed. Ceril, trancing nearby, heard that a conversation was taking place but could not make out its words; its contents were not automatically shared with the rest of the crew.

#### The Titans and the endangered ship

The approach to Broy's cliffs became increasingly rough. A storm threatened to overwhelm The Opal, forcing Kerben to concentrate on the helm while the crew, including [[Yalet Mora]], worked the ropes. Ceril's communion with the surrounding nature suggested that a moving Titan presence was intensifying the weather. Red Caesar located two approaching Titans, and Kerben steered toward the reported threat as the party tried to understand it.

[[Farron Acathian II]], a cloud Titan with dark blue skin, white hair and beard, and golden ornaments, rose beside the ship. A frost Titan, [[Zohai Lapis]], approached from the other side, carrying an icy bow. Their presence worsened the waves and brought punishing hail. Farron Acathian II was already injured before the encounter began.

Domyx recognized the significance of their nature. Unlike mortal Titanfolk, these beings could be driven by powers beyond their own will. Farron Acathian II addressed him as an abomination and his great-nephew, saying that Domyx had never been meant to descend from the homeland. His family connection did not avert the attack.

Domyx jumped from The Opal before combining his own enlargement with Red Caesar's magic. He grew large enough to face the Titans in the coastal water without putting his enormous weight aboard the ship. Ceril brought moonlight and guiding starlight against Farron Acathian II, while Vokenar summoned a celestial archer. The ship's consecrated deck helped protect those aboard from fear, but could not prevent the physical danger posed by the attackers.

Zohai Lapis supported her companion by transferring some of her vitality to him after he collapsed. Revived, Farron Acathian II turned to cloud and reappeared in the middle of The Opal. His arrival brought down a sail and threatened to capsize the vessel. Kerben tried to keep control, but the violent movement of the helm hurled him into the sea. Vokenar's celestial archer departed as the assault broke his focus.

With the ship tipping and its helmsman overboard, Domyx made saving it his priority. He gripped The Opal's sides and steadied the whole vessel with his enlarged strength. Ceril moved onto his shoulder, continuing to direct moonlight from that refuge. Keeping one hand on the ship, Domyx used [[Iklwa Isondo]] to bring Farron Acathian II down again and pull his body off the deck.

Zohai Lapis asked Domyx for permission to leave, saying that the victor could dismiss them and promising that they would do no further harm. Domyx relayed the request, but the party was uncertain what dismissal meant or whether the force ordering the attack could send them back. The confrontation continued until she too fell. Vokenar placed a divine guardian near Kerben in the water, offering him a protected means of getting out of it.

As the hail ceased and the sea began to settle, Domyx carried both fallen Titans to an exposed rocky promontory. Ceril helped locate a place they could reach before the enlargement ended, then climbed down as Domyx returned to his ordinary size. The danger to The Opal had passed, but the attackers' survival remained in the party's hands.

Farron Acathian II opened his eyes and handed Domyx a scroll case. Zohai Lapis gave up a long mithril chain. Domyx argued for sparing them: the attack had not been freely chosen, and killing them would not remove the power controlling them. Vokenar healed both Titans along with his injured companions.

The pair rose with their wrathful expressions unchanged, acknowledged that their task had failed, and walked north into the waters toward Domyx's homeland. They made no further attack. Their departure did not establish that they were free of their orders or would remain peaceful under future commands.

The scroll contained a map extending the party's knowledge of [[Thalassia]], including elven territory associated with the lands adjoining Broy, a northern landmass, and a passage through the hills toward the Titan homeland. Ceril studied it as navigator. Domyx knew how dangerous his descent had been and expected an ascent to be harder; outsiders did not ordinarily reach those heights. The map nevertheless supplied a route for a possible return.

#### Landing in Broy

With the storm abating, Broy's cliffs became visible again. The party expected to make land that day, with roughly fifteen days left under the deadline it then understood. The coastline was broken by rushing rivers and waterfalls fed both by flooded terrain and smaller holes in the sky.

Kerben found a concealed anchorage. He wanted to join the search for Obould, with whom he had the longest bond, but judged that someone needed to protect the captain's ship in hostile territory. He stayed with The Opal while Ceril, Domyx, Red Caesar, and Vokenar went ashore.

The four followed slippery rocks along the cliffs and reached a broad waterway. A wrecked vessel roughly the size of The Opal obstructed the current, and its fallen masts provided bridges across. As they crossed, they heard a frightened husband telling his wife to stay back while he protected her.

On the far side, a tiefling man and a human woman were surrounded by three [[Oni]]. The fiendish sentries looked almost identical, as though manufactured, with multiple horns and golden jewelry. Their linked golden manacles appeared to symbolize or enforce magical subjection rather than prevent them from moving.

Vokenar intervened before asking what had brought the couple into danger. He surrounded them with a ring of holy fire, separating them from their attackers, while Ceril brought down moonlight. The Oni identified the party as transgressors and began recording its assault on imperial guards.

Domyx used his newly developed divine battle cry to inspire his allies, his eyes and presence taking on a golden, reflective radiance. Vokenar spread his wings above the couple and released divine light against the fiends. The husband, sheltering his wife, watched the display with astonishment. Red Caesar prepared Steel Wind Strike as the battle continued.

#### Rescue and the wrong medicine

Red Caesar's Steel Wind Strike carried him through the Oni, cutting them while leaving the protected couple unharmed. Their wounded flesh broke into soot-like pieces, then drew back together through smoky regeneration. The sentries continued warning that the party's faces had been recorded and that assaulting palace Oni was a capital offense.

One Oni put Domyx into an enchanted sleep that ordinary attempts to wake him could not end. Vokenar drank the Brawny Bodak strength potion and struck the responsible fiend with his blade, breaking its hold and waking Domyx. Ceril defended himself with his enchanted staff, then moved inside the ring of protective light. The human woman used healing magic to help him.

Red Caesar unveiled Bigby's Hand, shaping a mechanical-looking palm with fingers of magical force. It held one Oni, crushed it, and dragged it into Vokenar's fire. The party overcame the fiends' regeneration through sustained attacks, and each dissolved into black ash or smoke when killed. Vokenar struck down the last one, dismissed the ring, and asked why the couple had been pursued. The woman offered further healing to their rescuers.

The husband, [[Trent Indorra]], admitted that he had stolen a bundle of medicinal herbs from the market. He and his wife, [[Ebbie Indorra]], had come from [[Southport]] seeking a cure for their children, but had realized too late that the stolen medicine would not help. He offered to guide the party into the city, although continuing deeper himself would bring more sentries after them.

Red Caesar took the herb bundle, and Ceril identified its purpose. It treated symptoms suffered by children who tried to cast too much magic before learning to control it. That condition would pass on its own; it was different from the [[Mana Sickness]] affecting the Indorras' family.

Their son was ill and their daughter had begun showing symptoms. They feared the disease was spreading rapidly through Southport, near the [[Mana Sea]], where rising waters had turned forest into dangerous swamp. Ceril knew that the remedy they needed was [[Deep Roses]], which grew underwater.

Red Caesar's medical knowledge supplied a grim warning. Mana Sickness was contagious and could leave victims with darkened eyes, their souls diffusing into the weave. Those approaching its final stage fell into an impenetrable slumber. There could still be a window of months in which to treat the sick, but the onset of that sleep would leave much less time. The rescuers could tell that Trent Indorra and Ebbie Indorra themselves were not presently infected.

The couple had made the long journey without a workable return plan. The wreck used to cross the river was their ship; they had hoped to repair it or build a raft from the pieces. Vokenar wanted to help once the party had dealt with its urgent business in Broy. The crew already had a reason to return someone to Southport, and he was prepared to accompany the Indorras separately if necessary rather than ignore their children.

Red Caesar objected to taking the ship into a plague-stricken town. His blunt description of the disease distressed the couple. Trent Indorra covered his wife's ears, then confronted him about whether the people who had just saved them would abandon them now. Ebbie Indorra appealed to Red Caesar's work as an abjurer, arguing that protection was his calling.

Ceril proposed a practical compromise: they could help the couple reach somewhere with transport, and he was willing to collect Deep Roses. Red Caesar accepted helping them travel without committing to entering Southport itself. The Indorras would show the party the entrance to the city, then return to the shore with a message for Kerben and wait aboard The Opal. The cure remained to be found.

While guiding them, Ebbie Indorra pressed a clear sapphire earring into Ceril's hand. She offered it as collateral and gratitude for their help, saying that he could keep it if he saved their people. The gift tied the newly accepted obligation to a family the party had already met and protected.

#### The sentries and Naomi Ue

The Indorras led the party through an opening in the hills and up winding stairs into an industrial settlement. Smoking pipes, turning gears, rushing water, and copper cables carrying electricity powered machinery throughout the town. Its industry exceeded even what the party had seen at [[The Pit]]. Trent Indorra stopped before the guarded entrance rather than risk another encounter with the Oni, and the couple headed back toward the ship.

Visible sentries watched from pillars and high positions. Vokenar used See Invisibility to discover further pairs concealed along the route through town. The Oni could hide themselves and might also use magic to detect invisible trespassers when alerted. Simple concealment would not solve every problem.

The signs were written in an eastern language the party could not read. Tongues could help with spoken conversation, but would not translate the writing. As they considered their options, Red Caesar recognized the stairs and archways from Vokenar's earlier vision of Obould being led into Broy. They were following the same route toward the capital.

The party wanted a place to rest and plan, rather than another fight with numerous sentries. Vokenar used divine intervention to conjure an Arcane Eye, preserving Red Caesar's magic in case invisibility became necessary. The eye let him see the town without exposing the group, though it carried no sound.

Through it, he found an active dive bar. Ordinary residents were talking and laughing; the Empire's suspicion of outsiders did not mean every inhabitant lived without enjoyment. He followed people carrying concealed documents into a back room, where a human woman with a white dog examined the papers and rang a bell.

An older, white-haired elf in armor arrived, applied a stamp using magically distinctive ink, and left. The papers then brought relief to their recipients, who exchanged money with the human woman. Vokenar inferred that the pair were using an official connection to remove people from legal trouble.

Before the eye faded, he traced a route back past the concealed sentries. With See Invisibility renewed, he led his companions to the tavern without needing Red Caesar's proposed invisibility. Once inside, their conspicuous arrival and billowing cloaks drew attention in the otherwise human crowd.

The woman he had observed introduced herself as [[Naomi Ue]]. Her white dog, Snow, seemed to approve of the visitors. Red Caesar began describing the missing captain, but Naomi Ue recognized enough to silence him and lead the group into the private room.

There, she asked what the orc had done. Red Caesar explained that he had loved the wrong woman and might have a complicated past, though he knew of no crime Obould had committed in Broy. Naomi Ue could remove many legal liabilities, but this case had attracted Vizier Jade's personal attention. She expected the captain to be held within the vizier's own chambers and warned that the party would have to deal with her directly.

Naomi Ue also explained the Empire's restriction of magic to those it trusted. Even the party's enchanted cloaks were beyond the privileges ordinarily available to visitors. Her own limited acceptance into the system had become a useful opportunity for her work.

She produced a magically written warrant bearing Domyx's likeness and describing the party. Oni observations automatically generated such records, and the instructions were shared with the other sentries. If they detected the party, they would act on the warrant. Clearing it would give the group a chance to travel openly instead of being attacked on sight.

Naomi Ue offered to have the record removed for gold or mithril. The party agreed and paid ten pounds of mithril. Vokenar also asked about redirecting the system against someone else or making the group appear to be residents, but those arrangements would require more time and wealth. They bought the cleared record and left the broader possibilities unused.

Naomi Ue rang the bell, and the armored elf returned. She stamped the paperwork, giving the party its clean slate. Then she looked up at Ceril and recognized him.

#### Vanzia Vynnfae and the imperial future

The elf was [[Vanzia Vynnfae]], who had escaped [[Brimbolyn]] with Ceril long before. Each had survived without knowing what had become of the other. Ceril said that he had spent much of the intervening time hiding; Vanzia Vynnfae had found a new place for herself within the Empire.

She wondered about [[Gilder Savar]] and the [[Rizolvir Kiirnodel]], the noble who had let them escape. Ceril offered to pass on her thanks to the latter. Vanzia Vynnfae wanted him to know that she had begun again somewhere she believed would prevent a return to the elven order they had fled. In her view, the strength of Broy would keep another [[Queen Caeradwyn]] from rising. Ceril cautioned that the future was never certain.

Ceril trusted her because she had once been the person he most needed to trust. After a brief discussion with his companions, he introduced them. Vanzia Vynnfae recalled that Ceril had placed his trust in the stars, and felt their reunion vindicated that guidance.

She confirmed Obould's situation. Her employer, Vizier Jade, held him as both a prisoner of war and leverage against [[The League of New Stark]]. The League was gathering forces and fighting the Empire; Vanzia Vynnfae expected Broy to prevail and thought the captain could be made an example because of his obligations to the League.

Ceril emphasized that they needed to speak to Obould about a matter affecting everything: the possibility of saving the sky. He put that conversation first, telling Vanzia Vynnfae that even if her employer intended to kill the captain, they needed access to him before it happened. Vanzia Vynnfae was skeptical that something so broken could be repaired. Ceril explained that he had aligned himself with [[Aeris]] and her plan, rather than accepting the Demi-Spell as the only answer.

Their discussion turned to Penumbra. Vanzia Vynnfae knew that Vizier Jade sought it and that the goddess also met with her. She teasingly claimed to be bound by an oath of absolute honesty and obliged to report everything she had heard. Her friends briefly took the claim seriously, and Red Caesar began bluffing the gestures of a destructive spell while she pretended to counter him.

They recognized the joke. The woman secretly erasing criminal records was not about to betray Ceril out of rigid obedience to an oath. No spell was cast in their exchange, and Ceril had no reason to believe she would report their confidence. Their awkward familiarity recalled the humor of the old escape companions, though her seasickness had made a life of travel with him unlikely.

Vanzia Vynnfae offered to lead them toward Obould, with the warning that Vizier Jade would know of their arrival and demand an audience first. She could also arrange citizenship papers by the morning, free of charge for a friend. She advised that remaining visitors might suit them better: citizenship brought greater scrutiny and made it easier for the authorities to follow or scry upon them.

Red Caesar chose citizenship. He wanted to understand Broy's political system, contrasting membership in a society one was born into with the League's freedom to come and go. He did not intend the paperwork as a promise to settle permanently. The others did not seek the same status.

Red Caesar also asked about buying spells, but learned that spell scrolls were contraband. Vanzia Vynnfae did not know a source for them, and neither did Naomi Ue. She did provide him with the scriptum for a Broyish teleportation circle farther up toward the capital.

Domyx asked about the Titanfolk living in the Empire. Vanzia Vynnfae explained that their homeland lay to the north; some had descended from it, while others had never fit into its society. She praised how well they had assimilated into Broy's industrial life. Their presence challenged the assumption that Titanfolk identity required remaining within the mountain culture.

#### A night in the capital and the Dawn Market

The route climbed toward the [[Broyish Capital]]. Horses and rentable wagons traveled beside self-propelled vehicles whose copper coils crackled with electricity. Airships moved overhead in numbers uncommon elsewhere, linking the metropolis with settlements scattered across distant mountains and plains.

The party chose to rest before attempting the palace. At the [[Dawn Market]], it found food and lodging amid a population more varied than Broy's reputation suggested. Humans predominated, but elves, half-elves, a tiefling, an orc, and many Titanfolk were present. Airships delivered goods, including fish, into a busy commercial world.

Their hotel and parts of the market were permanent dead magic zones. Other areas suppressed ordinary spellcasting while allowing the most powerful magic through. Those restrictions helped explain how the Empire's senior agents could enforce control while much of the population lacked a comparable means to resist. Vokenar could not assume that casting See Invisibility near a guard would be tolerated merely because their earlier warrants had been cleared.

After the overnight rest, Ceril watched the market waking. A child ran off with a toy airship, but an imperial official in smooth black armor and a gilded crown stopped him and held out a hand. The child returned the toy, and she settled the matter with the angry shopkeeper without an obvious arrest. Her active divinations and the invisible sentries made surveillance apparent, while the handling of the theft showed that enforcement could be discretionary.

The Oni changed watch at a precise time marked by the market's mechanical clock. A town crier announced intensified League attacks in the central region, imperial plans to respond to new southern allies, and the continued closure of the Titan strait. The party saw public news of the war without finding the emperor-worshiping posters Red Caesar had imagined.

The market offered weapons and magical ammunition, and Vokenar opened negotiations with a maker named Jeffrey to fit The Opal with cannons. The seller could send an airship down to the concealed vessel and complete the installation there. Vokenar thought it prudent to obtain the equipment while they could still trade freely, before the palace visit changed their situation.

While that arrangement proceeded, Red Caesar approached [[Qian Hu]], a merchant and mechanic whose shop displayed ancient artifacts beneath a great golden dragon. Qian Hu bowed and identified him as a wizard, traveler, and member of [[Heaven's Bulb]], claiming that Legend Lore had revealed who he was.

Qian Hu said that only worthy or important people could see him. He described an ancient world in which machines and magic worked together, claiming to be its sole surviving soul, carried through seventeen increasingly perfected forms. He had known [[Master Lee]] while using an earlier identity and said that he had teleported to freedom rather than waited for doors to open. Red Caesar recognized the connection but had little idea what to make of the extraordinary account.

The merchant traded in diamonds and legendary relics. The party's ordinary treasures and limited mithril were insufficient for his most exceptional wares, so Red Caesar made no purchase. Qian Hu invited him to return when he had greater resources.

#### Cannons and entry to the palace

The conversation with Qian Hu ended with a further hint that a special item could allow someone to reach a distant mountain. The party did not acquire that item or establish how the journey would work.

By the time Red Caesar returned, Vokenar had paid Jeffrey the mithril and installation crews had been dispatched. The Opal would receive cannons on both port and starboard sides. Making good use of them would require a master gunner to direct the weapons and a powder monkey to maintain and move their ammunition; those recruitment needs remained open.

Vokenar rode an airship down to check on the ship, which was still well hidden. Kerben briefly visited the market to trade with Jeffrey, but was eager to return to his responsibility aboard. He confirmed that Trent Indorra and Ebbie Indorra had arrived safely on The Opal.

Ceril, Domyx, Red Caesar, and Vokenar then approached the palace. An Oni watched from a minaret, and [[Fharan]], the seneschal, met them with an entourage of mostly masked humans. He took their names and distinguished Red Caesar, now a citizen, from the outsiders traveling with him.

Red Caesar said that they wished to ask about a prisoner. Fharan recognized the purpose of the request and subjected him to mind-reading, discovering more of his rescue intentions than he wished to reveal. The seneschal warned that liberating Obould would be difficult, but allowed the audience: Vizier Jade had once sent for Red Caesar and wanted to see him. Fharan's intrusion also gave them a warning of the greater power they would face inside.

#### Obould and the price of freedom

They entered Vizier Jade's chamber and saw [[Emperor Shen]] slumped on a magnificent throne. Metal and an elaborate headdress obscured his face, and he scarcely moved. He lifted his head at their arrival, prompting Vizier Jade to turn and greet the visitors.

Red Caesar made clear that they represented their ship and their captain, rather than appearing as League delegates. Vizier Jade treated the distinction as irrelevant to her price. The League had already been providing Penumbra, and every contribution extended the time she would keep Obould alive.

She openly intended to use the material to power [[Starfall]]. The party's previous divinations had connected the weapon to [[The Cataclysm]], making that demand much more than a ransom in ordinary treasure. Red Caesar challenged the exchange of their captain's freedom for fuel to destroy the world. Vizier Jade answered that she held the captain and the bargaining power; fairness was not her concern.

Red Caesar tried another approach. He claimed that Penumbra could be synthesized from something as plentiful as water, and offered the method in place of their supply. The Empire possessed industrial machinery that the party could not carry aboard The Opal. Ceril reinforced the performance by protesting that such dangerous alchemical secrets should not be given away.

The claim was a bluff. The party had theories about making Penumbra, but had not established a working process. Vizier Jade could read their thoughts and was unimpressed by an offer of knowledge she believed she could simply take from them. If they could manufacture the material, she asked why they had not brought it.

Ceril redirected the exchange toward proof that Obould was still alive. The party had seen him through divination, but needed to know that the other side of any proposed bargain still existed. Vizier Jade ordered guards to bring him out.

Obould appeared within a resilient sphere, still manacled and protected against escape by further magic. He looked gaunt and kept his head lowered, unwilling to meet his companions' eyes. Vizier Jade compounded the indignity by enchanting him to dance in front of them. His visible effort to turn away made the humiliation worse than a mere demonstration of custody.

Domyx's anger drew the emperor's attention. Emperor Shen studied him, then exposed his palms. They carried the same sky-colored mark as Domyx's hands, and his eyes resembled those of the younger Titanfolk. Vizier Jade identified the relationship: Emperor Shen was Domyx's grandfather.

The shared marks confirmed that he too had physically touched the sky. The revelation connected the emperor to the erased blood relative Ulrich Fjoller had warned Domyx about, and to the unnamed ancestor [[Sigil]] had identified as their great enemy.

When Red Caesar suggested parting with their available Penumbra to secure the captain, Obould finally raised his head. He fiercely warned them to give Vizier Jade nothing and began to explain the material's purpose. Before he could finish, his mouth sealed over with flesh. The guards returned him to the vizier's inner sanctum, where the party glimpsed the cage in which he was held.

Vizier Jade specified the terms: a piece of Penumbra would buy an extension of a week, while fifteen pieces would secure Obould's freedom. A sufficiently large piece might purchase more time. Red Caesar doubted that so much existed or could be gathered, but she insisted that the elves, the Empire, and the party's own travels demonstrated the available supply.

She left open the possibility of examining a manufactured sample if Red Caesar could actually produce one. No sample existed, no payment was made, and no release was agreed. Ceril and Vokenar would not accept feeding Starfall as the price of the rescue. Both began to withdraw from the audience.

#### Emperor Shen's purpose

Before they left, Emperor Shen forced himself to speak in the runic language understood by Domyx. He told his grandson that he had been the first of their bloodline to touch the sky, while Domyx was the last. He urged Vizier Jade to tell Domyx what he had done, taking responsibility and declaring that their shared rage would kill the gods.

Vizier Jade explained that touching the sky had changed Emperor Shen physically and mentally. He believed no one else should accomplish it after him, and had tried to destroy the sky so that he would be the last. The destruction had been incomplete; finishing it was the purpose behind their continued efforts.

Domyx recognized a warped form of the Akathian pursuit of singular excellence: his grandfather wanted to be the one person permitted that achievement. Red Caesar understood the emperor's selfish ambition but challenged the rest of the court for supporting a plan that would destroy their world and civilization.

Vizier Jade defended the cause in broader terms. She blamed the gods for the world's suffering, describing mortals as toys forced to enact the conflicts of divine factions. Destroying those powers, she argued, would end their control. Her account was an ideological justification, rather than proof that every disaster or conflict had been arranged by the gods.

Domyx challenged the notion that the gods all shared one purpose and remained appalled by the family's connection to the plot. Emperor Shen spoke once more, predicting that Stark would fall while they survived, and that the gods would die while they lived on. The effort left him unconscious. The party saw an exhausted, physically compromised emperor; the scene did not establish the promised survival or explain how it would be achieved.

Vizier Jade allowed the visitors to depart. As they left, she disclosed that further League deliveries had extended Obould's reprieve to roughly three months. The urgent deadline under which they had crossed the sea was no longer the whole situation. The League had bought the captain time by giving the Empire the same material the party needed to repair the sky.

#### The empty brig

Back together, the party considered its next move. Destroying Starfall had become a major priority, though Ceril doubted they could do it alone and Red Caesar suggested that resistance within Broy might help. Vokenar wanted at least to locate the weapon or learn more about it from people in the city.

Their earlier lore divination had been unable to reveal its location. That did not mean no person knew where it was. The capital's dead magic zones suggested one possible explanation for its concealment, but the party had not verified that theory or found the site.

Domyx also wanted to return to his homeland. The new map offered a route, and the identification of his grandfather made the unresolved family history much more pressing. With Obould's reprieve, they could consider that journey without the deadline they had expected on arrival. They planned to spend more time in Broy seeking information before settling the order of their commitments.

The pyramid remained an unvisited lead. The Indorras' medicine had not been found, the new cannon crew still needed recruitment, and Obould remained a prisoner. The meeting had supplied proof of life and the enemy's purpose without delivering a rescue.

The party returned to The Opal and spent the night aboard. While the others rested in the bunks, Vokenar took watch and checked the brig. Theo Harvey's cage was empty. The prisoner had vanished, and the session ended before the party learned when, how, or why.

### Summary

- Ceril located [[Tome Keeper's Pyramid]], and Vokenar learned that the [[Tome of Satariel]] captured souls and had been created from murdered wizards. The party postponed confronting its keeper.
- [[Theo Harvey]] privately told Domyx that [[The Karat Cake]] used Penumbra before the Cataclysm and that [[The Tyrant]] had failed to return from Vizier Jade's search for it.
- The party defeated [[Farron Acathian II]] and [[Zohai Lapis]]. Domyx saved The Opal from capsizing, Vokenar healed both Titans, and they departed after giving the party mithril and a map toward the Titan homeland.
- The party rescued [[Trent Indorra]] and [[Ebbie Indorra]], learned that their children needed [[Deep Roses]] for [[Mana Sickness]], and offered help. Ebbie Indorra gave Ceril a sapphire earring, and the couple reached The Opal safely.
- [[Naomi Ue]] and [[Vanzia Vynnfae]] cleared the party's imperial warrants for mithril. Ceril reunited with his old escape companion, Red Caesar obtained citizenship and a Broyish teleportation scriptum, and Vokenar paid to outfit The Opal with cannons on both sides.
- The palace audience revealed [[Obould]] alive but humiliated, and established [[Emperor Shen]] as Domyx's grandfather, the earlier sky-toucher responsible for trying to destroy the heavens.
- Vizier Jade demanded fifteen Penumbra pieces for Obould's freedom and disclosed League payments that had extended his reprieve to three months. The party gave her nothing.
- The party returned to The Opal to consider further investigation in Broy and a journey north. Vokenar then discovered Theo Harvey missing from the brig.

The voyage brought two very different threats into focus. The tome's history made Ceril's proposed use of its keeper morally and practically dangerous, while leaving the pyramid as a real lead for a later confrontation. The Titans' attack showed another form of subjection: powerful beings could carry out an order without the freedom Domyx valued. His decision to preserve both the ship and his defeated relatives kept their survival distinct from any claim that their compulsion had ended.

The Indorras introduced a responsibility that could not be reduced to the main struggle for Penumbra. Vokenar's immediate rescue grew into an offer to help their children, while Red Caesar's knowledge of the disease made him wary of exposing the crew. Ceril's compromise preserved a route toward assistance. The family was safe aboard the ship, but its wider crisis remained unresolved.

Broy complicated the party's expectations of the Empire. Its machinery, diverse residents, market trade, and occasional discretion existed alongside restricted magic, automatic criminal records, invisible sentries, and invasive surveillance. Vanzia Vynnfae viewed it as protection against renewed elven tyranny, and her friendship helped the party enter that system. Red Caesar's citizenship expressed curiosity about its workings, without committing him to its ruler's cause.

The audience made the central conflict personal to Domyx and exposed the cost of Obould's reprieve. Emperor Shen was the missing sky-touching ancestor, and his ambition transformed the family's ideal of excellence into a project against the gods. The captain's visible suffering sharpened the rescue obligation, but his interrupted warning opposed surrendering Penumbra to the weapon. Three more months offered room to act, while the League's payments showed that buying time could strengthen the enemy. The ship's new cannons improved its resources without resolving that dilemma, and Theo Harvey's disappearance added another urgent uncertainty to the closing situation.

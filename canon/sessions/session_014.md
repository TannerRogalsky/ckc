# Session 014

### Chunk 0000

[[chunks/session_014/chunk_0000|Source transcript]]

#### The jungle and the rains

Elven scouts escorted [[The Opal]] toward a landing on [[Continental Stark]], the large surviving remnant of the old world. [[Obould]] remained aboard to guard the ship while the party traveled inland toward [[Brimbolyn]]. The permission that had brought them through elven waters did not provide a direct entrance to the city: the coast and the dense jungle formed additional defenses. The scouts advised crossing the interior without spending a night there, warning that the creatures able to survive its hazards were formidable.

The rail network offered a possible route through the jungle. [[Ceril]] remembered helping lay its tracks with his fellow laborers before the trains themselves were operating. He recalled the destruction of forests and the loss of species during their construction, regarding the undertaking as an affront to nature. The continent had changed considerably since his escape, but finding a railway still seemed preferable to wandering through unfamiliar terrain.

Mist obscured the party's surroundings almost immediately. [[Vokenar]] used Locate Object to search for rails, guiding the group toward an old, rusted segment buried beneath vegetation and dirt. They chose to follow its course northward in the hope that it would meet a usable line. Ceril sent [[Nyquil]] ahead to scout; the owl could not see much through the canopy and fog, so Ceril sent the familiar on a longer search rather than recalling it at once. [[Domyx]] then noticed a slight pull on his lodestone greaves. Their magnetism allowed him to sense stretches of rail hidden underground, keeping the group on its path when the visible track disappeared.

A seemingly ordinary rainfall became a dangerous silvery downpour. The rain and the thickening fog burned the travelers with radiant energy before they reached shelter in a cave. Vokenar recognized the silver-white substance as [[Akasha]], resembling the liquid imbued into his sword and associated with the fallen oceans of [[Arkadia]]. The jungle's danger therefore carried a connection to the shattered heavens themselves. [[Red Caesar]] proposed using Leomund's Tiny Hut if another storm approached.

After the rain subsided, the party resumed its journey. Ceril found Nyquil dead on the ground, marked by burns and a lingering glow like those caused by the storm. He consumed the familiar's body. Almost immediately, Vokenar heard a large animal breaking through the foliage: a tyrannosaurus was approaching. Vokenar struck it with fire, but Ceril ended the immediate threat through Dominate Beast. His eyes and the animal's darkened as he took control, and he ordered it to run north and eat anyone resembling him. The beast departed along that course; the party did not establish what became of that order.

Ceril and Vokenar recognized the next Akasha storm early enough for Red Caesar to raise his shelter. His Tiny Hut appeared as a blue-tinted dome emanating from a central pillar adorned with gears and runes. The rain rolled off it while the party watched the surrounding fog fill with the same dangerous energy. Afterward, Akasha lay briefly on the ground like quicksilver before soaking into it. Vokenar collected some in a bottle despite the risk of burns, retaining a sample to investigate what the substance was and how it related to his blade.

#### The migrants in the bog

Further along the rail, the party encountered an armed elven scout fleeing south. She spoke modern Elvish, which only Ceril understood, and initially challenged the strangers while guarding a group in danger. A second scout appeared with news that the situation ahead had worsened. Migrants whom the scouts had been paid to escort were trapped in a bog, where a creature was devouring them. Ceril was wary of becoming involved in elven affairs, but told his companions that danger lay on their route.

Two fleeing civilians made the threat clearer. One spoke Western and explained that their families were still in the bog. They described a black-and-purple mound of mold with limbs, into which a companion had been partly absorbed. The other migrant was visibly sickened by what they had seen. They asked for help, while also explaining that the party could skirt the bog if it could not face the creature.

Red Caesar questioned the decision to intervene. The group still had difficult travel ahead, knew little about local politics, and could not assume that helping these migrants would improve its standing in Brimbolyn. Vokenar hoped that aid might open doors, but made clear that this was not his deciding reason: he would not leave people to die and would attempt the rescue alone if necessary. Red Caesar agreed to accompany him rather than risk losing his companion.

Water Walk allowed the party to cross the bog's quicksand without sinking. The first scout caught up and offered part of her payment, a hunk of mithril, as an immediate gift for their help. She also emphasized that the scouts were not members of [[The Order of Seasons]]. As the party advanced, Domyx pulled an unconscious migrant out of the mud and laid the survivor clear of it. Other people remained trapped nearby.

What first appeared to be another body stuck in the bog was instead attached to the [[Fuchsia Fungus]]. The creature rose out of the muck covered in purple growths, with its victim's flesh blending into its surface. Domyx attacked with his hammer, trying to avoid cutting the person embedded in it, while Vokenar called up a Guardian of Faith. Their intervention could not save that victim: the fungus drew the body fully into itself, spreading its flesh across its surface and incorporating its bones into its limbs. Another skull joined the animal skulls gathered where a head would have been. The party faced an enemy that could turn the trapped migrants into part of its own body.

### Chunk 0001

[[chunks/session_014/chunk_0001|Source transcript]]

#### Rescue and payment

The [[Fuchsia Fungus]] tried to draw [[Domyx]] into its tacky surface, but he pulled free. Its influence also dragged unconscious migrants through the mud toward it. [[Vokenar]] held one victim back by the clothing, while the elven scout waded into the bog to help with her musket. She repeated that she was trying to escape the continent rather than serve its ruling order, a distinction that recalled [[Ceril]]'s own earlier flight.

Ceril's moonlight and astral magic joined the guardian's radiance in burning the fungus. [[Red Caesar]] moved into the struggle and resisted its attempt to absorb him. His Thunderwave sent ripples through the bog that pushed endangered migrants away from the creature, interrupting its approach to its next victims. Domyx finally split the fungus with a hammer blow charged with holy light. Its purple and black substance turned to ash, leaving undigested remains to sink into the bog.

With the creature destroyed, Domyx carried unconscious survivors out of danger and handed them to the scouts. He considered the remains of those already consumed, but the immediate need was to get the living out of the jungle. Vokenar used healing light to restore the rescued migrants. Most of the group had survived, although the rescue had come too late for everyone.

The scout thanked Ceril and asked whether his powerful magic came from The Order of Seasons. His laughter answered the suggestion, and she was pleased that they did not share that allegiance. She explained that a junction lay several hours farther along the rail, with trains passing at each eighth of the sun. The next should arrive later that day. The scouts comforted their charges and resumed their attempt to reach the coast.

Before leaving, they gave Vokenar platinum credit chips from the Magic City. He passed them to Red Caesar to examine. The face of [[Queen Caeradwyn]] had been scratched off each chip, but the platinum retained most of its value. Red Caesar recognized a uniquely numbered series of currency whose use had declined after the queen's death. The payment connected the migrants' practical gratitude with the city's surviving traces of its former ruler.

#### Poisonous growths and an astral attacker

The party continued into wetter terrain, where standing water increasingly interrupted the broken rail. Some pools contained a heavier, silvery substance at the bottom, suggesting that Akasha could settle beneath ordinary water. This raised another possibility about what had become of Arkadia's fallen oceans, but the party did not resolve it. Domyx's magnetic sense continued to find the track through submerged and displaced segments.

The travelers then came upon translucent mushrooms whose shapes resembled full and crescent moons. Their inviting fragrance nearly drew Vokenar into sampling them, but his guarded mind resisted the impulse. Domyx's strict upbringing made him wary of unfamiliar food, and Ceril recognized the attraction as a natural danger. The mushrooms were poisonous and could cause blinding hallucinations. Ceril gathered them safely for [[Kerben]]'s poison research, adding another possible ingredient to the wyvern glands Kerben had recovered earlier. None of the party ate them.

An [[Aeon]] had been watching from a high ledge nearby, possibly waiting for the travelers to succumb to the mushrooms. It descended through the canopy in a hovering, meditative posture. Its green skin, finlike ears, and gold-trimmed armor marked it as unfamiliar to the party; its telepathic speech made its purpose more alarming. It described them as souls to drain and stars to bring home, flickers of light in a dying apocalypse.

The Githzerai's psychic attacks made the world seem to fade into the darkness between stars. Domyx and Red Caesar briefly perceived it as something they could not attack, even while it threatened the group. Vokenar used Calm Emotions to suppress that influence, allowing his companions to see and resist the attacker. The Aeon dispelled the protection, and their exchange became a struggle over both the party's minds and its defensive magic.

Red Caesar answered with lightning and counter-magic, Ceril with starlight, and Domyx by seizing the hovering enemy. The Aeon's shields were part of a psionic power drawn from an astral realm beyond Stark, Arkadia, and the immediate domain of their gods. Red Caesar used vampiric magic while Domyx held it, but the creature escaped his grip by teleporting and continued its attack. The encounter remained unresolved as it turned its attention toward Vokenar.

### Chunk 0002

[[chunks/session_014/chunk_0002|Source transcript]]

#### Mercy and departure

The [[Aeon]] continued attacking through its psychic distortions. Although its influence briefly affected [[Vokenar]], he could still protect and heal his companions. [[Domyx]] struck the hovering creature down with his hammer, while [[Ceril]] forced primal magic through its psionic shield. Their resistance left it badly weakened.

The Aeon then addressed Vokenar telepathically in a statement the whole group could hear. Whether they killed it or allowed it to leave, it said, they had earned a gem and coins hidden at the bottom of a nearby pool. Its next intention was to return to the stars. Vokenar accepted its word, told it to leave, and turned to healing [[Red Caesar]] rather than attacking again.

Red Caesar used Mage Hand to search the water and recovered a star gem together with unusual golden orbs used as astral coinage. The offering was real. Nevertheless, when the Aeon bowed and began Plane Shift, Red Caesar tried to counter its escape. It defeated his interference with a counterspell of its own. Before departing, it praised Vokenar's honor and condemned Red Caesar's conduct, associating the human with what it had heard of his people. The party retained the treasure and rested after the encounter; the Aeon's motives and broader mission remained unknown.

#### Brim the Bullywog and the railway guards

Continuing along the track, the party reached the point where the neglected rail had once joined a newer network. The old junction had been removed, but the active line was clean, shiny, and protected from debris. Nearby stood a moss-covered hut whose frog-headed inhabitant mistook the arrivals for a train bringing supplies. He introduced himself as [[Brim the Bullywog]] and directed them toward a depot, warning that the Order's knights were on heightened alert because of the [[Broyish Empire]].

Brim the Bullywog wanted to leave the jungle and see more of the world. He had built contraptions from salvaged or stolen railway parts, but disliked relying on theft and deception to survive. He wanted productive work and better materials. His inventions included a pair of mechanical wings that had never managed to fly; he admitted selling a similarly unsuccessful machine to someone in Brimbolyn. Red Caesar inspected the workshop and found that its many failed experiments concealed substantial skill. With limited scrap, Brim the Bullywog had produced some successful devices and showed both ability and persistence.

The party offered him work aboard The Opal as a tinkerer and lookout. Vokenar described its spotlights and other improvements, suggesting that Brim the Bullywog could help develop its defenses and perhaps counter the Empire's new airborne technology. Brim the Bullywog demonstrated an extraordinary leap, explaining that he could often escape danger simply by jumping beyond its reach. He accepted the invitation on the condition that the party keep supplying metal and projects so that he could practice his craft. He hoped eventually to build something that would rival the engineers of the East.

Brim the Bullywog accompanied them to the depot, where broad clearings and magically altered land again reminded Ceril of the railway's cost to the jungle. Cloaked members of the [[Knights of the Four Seasons]] approached, wearing the Order's emblem: a full moon flanked by its waning and waxing phases. Their tiefling officer, [[Marshal Zem]], questioned the travelers about connections to the Empire. He singled out Domyx's resemblance to people said to live in Broy, but the group denied imperial service.

The knights inspected their equipment and used separate auguries to judge whether each traveler could safely enter the city. Satisfied, they placed an augmented Sanctuary upon them, offering protection against aggression that would be forfeited by an attack of their own. Brim the Bullywog vouched for the party and submitted to the same familiar procedure. Despite his earlier concern about returning to town, Marshal Zem knew him and admitted him.

The marshal described an extensive network connecting the Magic City to surrounding towns and noble courts, including [[House Kiirnodel]]. Its leader, [[Rizolvir Kiirnodel]], was the son of [[Aramil Kiirnodel]] and a prominent supporter of the Order. New railways were still being laid to bring outlying villages into that network and into agreement with the city's ways. Marshal Zem could offer little advice about lodging, but warned that magi might watch the visitors during their stay.

A magically conducted train took the party into Brimbolyn far faster than a ship could have carried them. Its few other passengers included elves and a pixie, who briefly landed on Domyx's head before realizing that he was not a rock. In the city, trams and trains linked the market, noble estates, and [[The Academy]], the center of the Order's work. Brim the Bullywog arranged to meet the group again at midnight, marked by a city bell, before wandering off on his own.

#### Survivors and trades in the Magic City

The party separated to explore. Red Caesar sought the golden-headed brother of [[Yalet Mora]], while Vokenar, Ceril, and Domyx visited the [[Elvish Marketplace]] and learned about the city around them. Elves and tieflings predominated. Humans attracted conspicuous attention, and Domyx was also an unusual sight.

Vokenar met [[Osiris Dims]], an aasimar born on Stark rather than in Arkadia. His mother had fallen from the heavens and found kindness with his half-elf father. Osiris Dims was now forty-five and had not seen either parent in some time. His father had become ill, and the couple's opposition to the Order had prevented them from remaining in the city; Osiris Dims thought they might have gone to [[Southport]], but did not know. They had wanted him to have a better life, and he saw remaining in Brimbolyn as a way of honoring them.

Vokenar was delighted to discover more survivors and descendants of his people, making clear that mixed ancestry did not diminish that welcome. Osiris Dims had seen other aasimar in town and mentioned a merchant named [[Gossa]]. He also described the Magic City and The Order of Seasons as inseparable. In his understanding, some past wrongs had left no good path forward and therefore had to be undone. He had heard that newly acquired resources might allow the [[Demi-Spell]] to be cast in less than a year, but directed Vokenar to the Academy for an explanation of how it worked.

At the market, Gossa seemed familiar to Vokenar before he could place her. She recognized him immediately as a former pupil at [[Academia Lux]]. She had remained in Arkadia when so many angels fell, then traveled below in the hope of finding survivors. Vokenar explained that the gods had saved him and sent him to help repair the sky, while withholding details he was not yet ready to share in the city.

Gossa had become an art collector, preserving beauty from the old world and trading magical goods made through the skills she had learned in Arkadia. She trusted the Order's promised solution but admitted that she understood little of its technical basis. Its members kept many secrets. What she had been taught was that the Demi-Spell was the only available solution, even if it was not a good one, and that souls possessed a permanence beyond the changes it would cause.

Vokenar worried about the people who had been born since [[The Cataclysm]]. If the past changed, someone such as Osiris Dims might never have existed in the same way. Gossa acknowledged the risk and urged him and Ceril to learn more rather than assume that the Order had to be their enemy. She also warned that opposition had ended badly for others. Decades earlier, someone claiming good intentions had tried to destroy everyone who knew the Demi-Spell and sought further flooding of the world. Vokenar wondered whether that person was [[Morel Chainsunder]], but the conversation did not establish the identity.

Domyx meanwhile visited [[Lesanderol Nokiirna]], a mithril smith whose family craft stretched back into the old world. Domyx respected the artisan's dedication, and the smith examined both his weapons and his magnetic greaves. In exchange for mithril, his existing trident, gold-trimmed books, and a piece of pure gold, Domyx acquired the [[Iklwa Isondo]]. Its lightweight mithril construction and an iron coil allowed it to return to his lodestone greaves when thrown and to pull an impaled target toward him. Domyx stayed to watch the finishing work and test the new weapon.

Elsewhere in the city, Red Caesar entered an alley where young elves gathered around him. Their talk singled him out as human. One cast Sleep, exploiting a vulnerability that made the spell a racial taunt in an elven neighborhood. The spell failed to put Red Caesar to sleep. As the youths fled, he teleported behind the caster and slapped the back of his head.

### Chunk 0003

#### The golden-headed brother

An older elf intervened in the alley, chastising the young caster and apologizing for his son. He introduced himself as [[Garsynth Feralol]]. His son was neglecting his studies and causing mischief despite hopes of rising within [[The Order of Seasons]]. Garsynth Feralol warned [[Red Caesar]] that humans received little welcome in some parts of town and that the surrounding alleys were frequented by gangs. He was surprised that a human had secured admission to the city at all.

Red Caesar explained that he and his friends wanted to undo the world's damage, though through a different approach from the Order. Garsynth Feralol believed that the elves had more allies abroad than their leaders acknowledged. Red Caesar added that repairing harm should not require erasing the good that had grown during the last century. Their conversation offered a more hospitable exchange after the youths' hostility.

Asked about [[Yalet Mora]]'s golden-headed brother, Garsynth Feralol described a living stone monument outside the city. Children sometimes ventured there despite warnings against entering the jungle. He gave Red Caesar directions by train and then through a relatively safe stretch of forest, cautioning him to watch for the burning rains.

Red Caesar made the first visit alone. A misty valley led between steep cliffs and past stone creations, including a mango and a miniature train, that showed the work of a powerful earth-shaper. At its end stood a vast sculpted face with shrubs and trees for hair. A booming voice emerged through the structure and introduced [[Yalet Aurum]], whose golden body was enclosed within the monument. When Red Caesar explained his wish to see the famed head, Yalet Aurum opened the monument's mouth and displayed the glow of gold within it.

Yalet Aurum knew of [[Penumbra]], but said it was among the few stones over which he had no power. He heard what the surrounding mountain heard and knew that the Order was interested in gathering the fragments. Anyone seeking them would need to act faster than an elf.

He then challenged Red Caesar with a riddle about Yalet Mora. Claiming to make only mountains while his brother made only small rocks, he asked what Yalet Mora could make a visitor that would grant courage. Red Caesar could not find the answer and asked permission to return with companions better suited to the puzzle. Yalet Aurum agreed while laughing at his difficulty, and Red Caesar withdrew to seek help.

The party returned together. [[Ceril]] proposed that small stones could provide the minerals used in makeup, giving someone confidence in a way a mountain could not. Yalet Aurum appreciated this unexpected answer and offered Ceril a chunk of gold as a piece of his mind. Red Caesar then found the intended answer: Yalet Mora could make someone “bolder,” echoing “boulder.” Yalet Aurum's laughter produced more gold, which the party collected. He thanked them for amusing him and asked them to tell his brother that he remained small and worthless.

#### A new form for Ceril's familiar

Back at the [[Elvish Marketplace]], Ceril returned to [[Gossa]] with a request prompted by the loss of [[Nyquil]]. He wanted a way to make his familiar more resilient. Gossa offered an amulet through which he could summon the familiar as an elemental, and Ceril chose magma. She fashioned a necklace around a faintly glowing piece of volcanic rock. Summoned through it, the owl would have claws, fire breath, and an explosive burst upon death.

Ceril traded the mask of Ogun for the amulet. Gossa hung the artwork behind her shop, accepting it as the sort of old-world piece she wished to collect. [[Vokenar]] considered a purchase as well, but declined when it became clear that his available goods did not suit her preferred trade.

#### The spell and the choice of a world

The party traveled by tram to [[The Academy]], whose warm-lit buildings stood amid jungle growth rather than replacing it. Nature had reclaimed the walls and wildlife moved freely through the grounds. As they arrived, Ceril recognized two figures on a balcony: [[Keys Caeradel]] and [[Illidrielle Gandara]], the tiefling and half-elf who had taken Penumbra from [[Castle Kaedon]]. They withdrew from the balcony to come downstairs and meet the visitors.

The human [[Stephanne Quist]] welcomed the party at the entrance. Vokenar asked for information about the Demi-Spell, and the visitors were led to its researchers. Illidrielle Gandara recognized Ceril from their earlier encounters and invited the group to ask its questions. Keys Caeradel had been writing the spell; other members of the Order helped develop and copy the work.

Keys Caeradel explained that descriptions of rewriting the past simplified what the project actually did. The spell would carry the world forward through an immense stretch of future time until everything began again. Souls and elements would be recycled. Some people and places were expected to recur, while others would not. Chaos would prevent the new world from simply repeating every event of the old one, although that did not guarantee a future without suffering.

Vokenar saw a difficult moral trade. The spell might rescue lives lost in the past and prevent the old catastrophe, but it could also undo lives born afterward. Repairing the existing sky offered a future to people already living. He questioned which approach would cause less harm and whether the same disaster might eventually recur. Keys Caeradel argued for the most positive effect rather than merely the least change, and challenged the party's assumption that trusting the gods was a safer gamble.

Illidrielle Gandara said that the Demi-Spell was the one mortal undertaking powerful enough to subject even the gods to its effects. She questioned whether those powers were themselves part of the problem, suggesting that [[Aeris]] might have helped cause the Cataclysm. Vokenar acknowledged that he could not rule out divine responsibility, but neither could the accusation be proved. The party had not learned the catastrophe's full cause.

Red Caesar centered his objection on choice. He wanted to repair the sky so that people could decide what to do with their lives, rather than choose a replacement world on their behalf. Keys Caeradel challenged him in return: mortal magic already changed the world, and the gods' absence had left mortals to make judgments about right and wrong. Illidrielle Gandara also argued that repairing the sky imposed a choice. She cited [[The League of New Stark]]'s reported belief that flooding selected for those able to survive, while explicitly rejecting that philosophy herself. Red Caesar refused the suggestion that these choices were equivalent in their consequences and declared that he would resist the forced renewal.

Vokenar tried to move the discussion from confrontation toward an understanding. He asked who had participated in the decision. The researchers described agreement among the elven houses and much of the continent's population, including humans. That answer established their local support, not consultation with every surviving people. Their alliance was not based on easy affection: Keys Caeradel said he disliked many of the elves, and had nevertheless joined them for the shared purpose. House Kiirnodel was a principal source of power and support behind the growing spell.

Asked how the risks were being checked, the researchers described Keys Caeradel's extensive divinations, repeated reviews, and records of anything departing from their established process. They brought the party into his laboratory. The spell filled volumes and stacks of paper rather than a single ordinary scroll. Lists of plants and animals described things that ought to exist in the next world, linked through symbols and mathematical structures. Elven, tiefling, and aasimar scribes all worked on it.

Stephanne Quist explained their expectation that a new world would follow a different course, eventually encountering its own wars, famines, plagues, or catastrophe after a long period of renewed life. Worlds began in peace and declined toward entropy. [[Genesis Mundi]] marked the end of one and the beginning of another; the Order sought to hasten that transition. This remained their explanation for preferring renewal over preserving a world they judged exhausted.

Penumbra provided the energy to advance the project without draining as much of its casters' own lives. The spell could harm people who committed their souls to its casting, and some researchers had already died without seeing its completion. Others now hoped that the fragments would bring the work within reach during their remaining lifetimes. Their urgency therefore combined concern for the world with the limits of the people conducting the research.

Keys Caeradel also rejected Red Caesar's accusation that he simply wished to become a god. He said he had already refused that path and considered himself a scientist trying to save the world. His father's power could not solve the catastrophe in the way Red Caesar proposed; if he knew a less disruptive answer, he would pursue it. The researchers demanded evidence for the party's alternative. Their own use of Penumbra had visibly advanced their spell, whereas they had not seen the party return even one fragment to the heavens. Red Caesar, distrustful after the theft at Castle Kaedon, was unwilling to reveal every part of the process.

#### An accord and a family request

Stephanne Quist suggested allowing the party to attempt its repair before the Demi-Spell was ready. If the sky ceased to be broken, the Order might have less reason to proceed. Red Caesar argued that Penumbra was essential to the repair plan but only an accelerator for the researchers' project. Taking it away from the party could therefore prevent one possible salvation merely to speed another.

Illidrielle Gandara found enough force in that argument to propose a temporary pause in the Order's collection of Penumbra. She wanted evidence that even one piece of sky could be restored before placing greater trust in the plan. [[Domyx]] questioned the decision to end a universe before its course had played out and the possibility of replacing it with something worse. He also challenged the assumption that the gods had deliberately allowed the catastrophe. The discussion distinguished their great power from an ability to do absolutely anything.

Keys Caeradel accepted a limited accord: the Order would stop collecting new Penumbra for a time, but retain what it had already gathered until the party provided assurances. Vokenar offered a reciprocal condition. If the party's repair strategy failed, it would give its collected Penumbra to the Order to help power the Demi-Spell. Both sides could pursue an answer without immediately obstructing one another. Ceril and Keys Caeradel shook hands, and Illidrielle Gandara proposed granting the visitors access to the teleportation circles.

Before the party left, Keys Caeradel asked for help with a personal matter. He described his father, the [[Rakshasa]], as a god whom he had spent years visiting in a cave without feeling that he truly knew him. He wanted the party to bring his father to the Academy. Red Caesar revealed that he had met the Rakshasa at [[The Garden]], where the being had seemed guarded and disinclined to move. He had previously treated that encounter as a meeting with an old recluse rather than shared its possible importance. The family request gave the party another reason to return to the Garden.

### Chunk 0004

[[chunks/session_014/chunk_0004|Source transcript]]

#### House Kiirnodel and the shape of renewal

At the suggestion of [[Keys Caeradel]] and [[Illidrielle Gandara]], the party visited [[House Kiirnodel]], where Keys Caeradel's mother lived. Pink, purple, and blue flowers covered the estate. [[Ceril]] immediately recognized [[Feronia Caeradel]] and [[Rizolvir Kiirnodel]] as the two people who had confronted his escaping group before [[The Cataclysm]]. They had been able to stop him, yet allowed him to continue. He was astonished to find them alive.

The hosts did not initially recognize him. Feronia Caeradel introduced herself as a drow of former House Caeradel, Keys Caeradel's mother, and a principal authority responsible for enacting the Demi-Spell. Ceril explained their earlier meeting and said that for years he had wondered whether they had been a mirage or an appearance of nature rather than people.

Feronia Caeradel then identified him by name and said she had recognized his future. She believed that his soul survived many worlds unchanged and that he had never been meant to remain bound. Given the years ahead as a free elf, she thought he could become like a god. Her words offered an explanation for their earlier restraint and a prediction about his potential; they did not establish a future transformation.

Rizolvir Kiirnodel warned that the party's greatest enemy would be the [[Broyish Empire]]. It opposed the Order's effort to bring about Genesis Mundi and was gathering Penumbra of its own. His intelligence suggested that the fragments were intended for a weapon, an interpretation he considered plausible because of the Empire's dedication to war. The exact use remained unknown. He described an ambitious power willing to kill people who merely came to its attention, even when they were not actively obstructing it.

The discussion of elven and human violence also recalled Queen Caeradwyn's influence over [[Kaedon]]. Her bloodline had conferred magical power while carrying a madness that helped doom the old kingdom. That history complicated any easy claim that elven rule had been less destructive simply because its violence took a different form.

Rizolvir Kiirnodel said he was certain of very little beyond the Demi-Spell, into which he had poured his life. The hosts also compared the communities protected by the Empire with those preserved by the Order. Many humans depended on the imperial system for survival and shared its determination; their shorter lives could sharpen ambitions beyond those of elves. This complicated the warning by distinguishing the Empire's people from the regime that shaped their choices.

[[Vokenar]] asked how Genesis Mundi differed from the Demi-Spell. Feronia Caeradel first described them as names for the same undertaking, then made the distinction explicit: Genesis Mundi was the natural fixed point between the end of a world and the creation of the next. It would occur eventually regardless of mortal action. The Demi-Spell would expedite that transition and add guidelines intended to shape the new world's creation. The two were connected, but the spell was a means of influencing the larger event.

Vokenar also asked whether those guidelines would treat every people fairly, including the humans of the Empire. The hosts assured him that their intent was fairness. They did not believe that every world needed a regime like Broy's; people whose minds had been shaped by imperial propaganda could receive another chance to choose differently. Vokenar judged the exchange largely forthright, while recognizing that leaders might still hold some information close.

Before the visit ended, the hosts asked the party to check on [[Boril Erendel]] at the Garden. He was an Order member who had spent a long time away working on his own project, and they worried about where his thinking and private plans had led. They wanted him persuaded to return to House Kiirnodel for a conversation. Ceril already knew him as the person who had helped secure the party's permission to enter elven waters.

Feronia Caeradel supplied the promised [[Teleport Keys]], the encodements needed to target the house's teleportation circle. Using them would still require the appropriate spell or a scroll; the agreement supplied a destination and permission to return. The party had learned the Order's intentions and gained a way to maintain contact without accepting its proposed renewal as inevitable or right.

#### Departure and Vizier Jade's promise

After resting, the party returned to its seaward journey. [[Cutlass Cray]] was chosen as the next stop, with further shopping, the Garden, and a return to [[Lyngbakr Lagoon]] still within the planned circuit. The new tinker's possible work on The Opal's defenses was discussed, but no completed upgrades were established during this session.

A brief scene away from the party showed Keys Caeradel draining energy from a Penumbra fragment. The Demi-Spell's scroll physically lengthened and its existing writing glowed more brightly, providing a direct view of the progress the Academy's researchers had described. The pause in collecting new fragments had not ended their work with the energy already held.

On the first evening after departure from Continental Stark, [[Red Caesar]]'s alarm drew him onto [[The Opal]]'s deck. [[Vizier Jade]] had returned to ask whether he had considered her invitation to Broy. He said the winds were carrying the party in that direction, but admitted that his companions might be more willing to visit if they did not know of his private invitation. He would struggle to explain it to them.

Vizier Jade imposed no deadline and reiterated that their ship would be allowed through imperial waters. She had informed [[Emperor Shen]] of the anticipated visit. She distinguished the military's effort to map Stark from her own attention to individuals who might rise to great power, including Red Caesar. She said she had been watching him, would continue to keep him alive, and believed that leaving Obould unchecked would bring disaster. Those were her claims and her assessment of the captain.

Red Caesar objected to having her influence weigh upon his life. If she had truly observed him, he argued, she should know that subtlety and his own decisions mattered. His study of abjuration had to mean that he could protect himself if he was ever to help protect the world.

Vizier Jade acknowledged that her effort to shape his ambition might have become too intrusive. She offered to stop watching and trust him to survive on his own. As a stronger assurance, she handed him a Geas scroll with which he could restrict her conduct and prevent further surveillance. Red Caesar returned it uncast. Her willingness to offer the bond was enough for him to extend trust, which he expected her to return.

She accepted that exchange, saying that his eventual arrival in the capital would vindicate her trust. She promised that he would not see her again until then and departed through a teleportation circle. The session closed with their invitation still private and the relationship resting on a spoken commitment rather than an enforced magical restriction.

### Summary

- The party followed old railway tracks across [[Continental Stark]], survived [[Akasha]] storms, lost [[Nyquil]], and retained an Akasha sample for investigation.
- [[Vokenar]] insisted on rescuing trapped migrants despite [[Red Caesar]]'s objections. [[Domyx]] killed the [[Fuchsia Fungus]], the party saved most of its intended victims, and the scouts rewarded their aid with mithril and platinum credits.
- [[Ceril]] gathered poisonous moonlike mushrooms for [[Kerben]]'s research. An [[Aeon]] attacked the party, then escaped after offering a star gem and astral gold; Vokenar granted mercy while Red Caesar tried to prevent its departure.
- The party recruited [[Brim the Bullywog]] as a tinkerer and lookout, passed [[Marshal Zem]]'s inspections, and entered [[Brimbolyn]] by magical train.
- Vokenar met [[Osiris Dims]] and reunited with [[Gossa]], learning about other aasimar survivors and the risks attributed to the [[Demi-Spell]].
- Domyx acquired the [[Iklwa Isondo]]. Ceril later exchanged the mask of Ogun for an amulet that could summon his familiar as a magma elemental.
- [[Yalet Aurum]] confirmed his knowledge of [[Penumbra]], challenged the visitors with a riddle about [[Yalet Mora]], and rewarded their answers with gold.
- At [[The Academy]], [[Keys Caeradel]] and [[Illidrielle Gandara]] explained the world-renewing project. The Order agreed to pause new Penumbra collection; the party promised its own collection if repairing the sky failed.
- Keys Caeradel asked for a reunion with the [[Rakshasa]]. The hosts at [[House Kiirnodel]] requested a check on [[Boril Erendel]] and supplied [[Teleport Keys]].
- [[Feronia Caeradel]] and [[Rizolvir Kiirnodel]] connected Ceril to his escape before the Cataclysm. They clarified [[Genesis Mundi]] and warned of the [[Broyish Empire]]'s suspected use of Penumbra for a weapon.
- After departure toward [[Cutlass Cray]], [[Vizier Jade]] returned privately to Red Caesar. She agreed to end her surveillance, and he declined to enforce that assurance through a spell.

The journey made the division between care for the living and plans for the world's future concrete. Vokenar chose to help endangered strangers before he knew whether that choice would benefit the mission, and his reunion with people from Arkadia gave him living examples of what a reset might undo. Red Caesar's caution did not keep him from the rescue, but his later dispute with the researchers made his commitment to individual choice explicit. Their differing responses to the Aeon's offer also showed that the party did not share a single understanding of mercy or trust.

Brimbolyn presented both preservation and control. Its guarded railways, protective enchantments, and Academy sheltered communities and enabled ambitious magical work. Its surveillance, hostility toward a human visitor, and exclusion of Osiris Dims's parents showed the limits of that welcome. Ceril encountered a continent altered since his escape, yet found an Academy that allowed nature within its walls and two former pursuers who had chosen to spare him. The meeting complicated his relationship with elven society without settling it.

The Academy debate changed the Penumbra conflict from immediate competition into a conditional arrangement. The researchers retained their existing resources and their belief in renewal, while the party gained time to demonstrate another answer. No fragment was restored to the sky in this session, no Demi-Spell was cast, and the accord did not establish agreement over which outcome was morally preferable. Its practical effect was a pause in the Order's collection and a promise that failure would make the party's resources available to the alternative.

At the close, the Garden held both a family request and concern about an absent researcher. The Empire's purpose for its Penumbra remained a suspicion, while its interest in Red Caesar had become personal and explicit. His exchange with Vizier Jade extended the same concern with self-direction into their private relationship: he accepted trust while refusing a bond imposed by magic.

A close friend and loyalist of [[Clan Akathia]] who risked exile or execution by following [[Domyx]] down from the titan mountains. He warned [[Domyx]] that another blood relative from [[Clan Akathia]] had touched the sky before him, had been erased from the records, and still existed somewhere in Stark.

## Plot Events

In session 016, [[Lorelai Lapis-Acathian]] revealed in a dream to [[Domyx]] that Ulrich had been imprisoned in dungeons for months after returning from seeking Domyx on the hill. He refused to say why he went, but Lorelai Lapis-Acathian believed it was to speak with Domyx about something deeply tied to clan ancestry and bloodline secrets. The clan sentenced Ulrich to execution, and Lorelai Lapis-Acathian was pregnant with his child — a fact that would soon become impossible to hide. Domyx pledged to rescue him.

In session 024, [[Charlotte]] revealed that Ulrich had told her before being taken away that what he discovered about the clan would bring it crumbling down — that [[Emperor Shen]] was "some kind of a monster" but Ulrich never had time to research what specifically. Ulrich was held in the [[Prison of Frost]], not far from [[Acathian Manor]]. [[Lorelai Lapis-Acathian]] confirmed this location and indicated that [[Domyx]] would know how to reach it.

In session 024, the party reached the [[Prison of Frost]], a facility sealed behind a plane of pure magical energy — an abomination to titan folk who traditionally shun magic. [[Domyx]] presented himself without his Akathian title as a mark of his renounced identity. [[Ceril]] dispelled the barrier with ease, freeing Ulrich. He greeted Domyx and [[Lorelai Lapis-Acathian]] with relief and asked if the world below was safe for them. With the barrier broken, Ulrich and Lorelai Lapis-Acathian joined the party and returned to [[The Opal]] as new crew members.

In session 031, Ulrich manned one of [[The Opal]]'s cannons during the [[Broyish Empire]] naval attack and advised [[Kerben]] on ship tactics while the crew fought to escape the incoming fleet.
